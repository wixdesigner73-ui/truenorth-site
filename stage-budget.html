The problem

Budgets blow out when quotes are based on incomplete drawings, vague specifications and allowances that are only a guess.

Why it matters

Builders can only price what is documented. A detailed specification and schedules let you compare tenders “apples with apples”.

What this stage covers

  • Spreadsheet

Practical steps

How to approach budget

  1. Set an early ballpark

    Use a preliminary estimate before the design is final, so the house is drawn to your budget.

  2. Break it into cost centres

    Site works, slab, frame, roof, windows, services, fit-out. Each can be checked and tracked.

  3. Write a specification

    Spell out materials, finishes and inclusions. What the specification leaves out tends to come back later as a variation.

  4. Watch the allowances

    PC items and provisional sums are estimates. Low allowances make a quote look cheaper than it is.

  5. Keep a contingency

    Hold money back for the unexpected, especially for renovations and difficult sites.

Common mistakes

What catches people out

  • Comparing quotes that aren’t alikeDifferent inclusions make the cheapest price misleading.
  • Low provisional sumsThey are often where the extra costs appear.
  • No contingencyEvery project meets something unexpected.

Before you rely on this: Prices vary by location and over time. Treat any estimate as a guide and confirm costs with your builder and suppliers.
