The problem

Most people find a block or a house they like first, and check whether it suits their plans later. By then the deposit is paid and the problems are theirs.

Why it matters

The land decides what you can build, how the house will sit in the sun, and much of what it will cost. A steep, shaded or heavily constrained block can add significant cost before a single wall goes up.

Practical steps

How to approach find

  1. Decide your path

    Build new, buy and renovate, or knock down and rebuild. Each path needs different checks, so be clear before you start looking.

  2. Check what the land allows

    Ask council for the planning certificate and check zoning, easements, flood, bushfire and other overlays before you commit.

  3. Look at the block with the sun in mind

    Note where north is, the slope, views, access, drainage and what the neighbours could build later.

  4. Look at a house with renovation in mind

    Check structure, condition and layout. Ask whether it can be improved affordably, or whether knocking down is the better choice.

Common mistakes

What catches people out

  • Buying on the view aloneA view to the south can mean living areas that never see winter sun.
  • Ignoring the slopeEvery metre of fall adds cost to footings, retaining walls and access.
  • Skipping the planning certificateEasements, overlays and building envelopes can rule out the house you have in mind.

Before you rely on this: Planning rules differ between states and councils. Always confirm requirements with your local council and get professional advice for your property.
