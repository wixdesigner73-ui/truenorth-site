The problem

Many owners choose on price alone, then discover what wasn’t included once the contract is signed.

Why it matters

The contract sets out who pays for what, when, and what happens when things change. Reading it carefully is far cheaper than a dispute.

What this stage covers

  • Contractor pre-qualification
  • Tender process
  • Contracts
  • Progress payments
  • Variations

Practical steps

How to approach build

  1. Pre-qualify builders

    Check licences, insurance, recent work and references before you invite them to price.

  2. Tender with full documents

    Give every builder the same drawings, specification and schedules so the prices can be compared.

  3. Compare quotes line by line

    Look at inclusions, exclusions, allowances and the construction period, not just the total.

  4. Read the contract before you sign

    Understand the payment schedule, the variations process, insurances and dispute steps.

  5. Keep everything in writing

    Record decisions and variations as they happen during construction.

Common mistakes

What catches people out

  • Choosing on price aloneThe cheapest quote is often the least complete.
  • Verbal variationsIf it isn’t written down and priced, it will be argued about later.
  • Paying ahead of progressPayments should follow the stages set out in the contract.

Before you rely on this: Building contracts are legal documents. Get independent legal advice before you sign.
